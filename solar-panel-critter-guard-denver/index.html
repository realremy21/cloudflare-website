Why Denver arrays get tenants

Denver's pigeon population is established and year-round, our winters are sunny enough that birds stay active instead of migrating, and the under-array space stays noticeably warmer than the open roof. Neighborhoods with mature trees — Washington Park, Park Hill, the older suburbs — see heavy pressure, but new builds aren't spared: an unguarded array in a brand-new subdivision is simply the first open listing on the street. Once one pair nests successfully under a system, they return, their young return, and the debris compounds every season it goes unaddressed.

The calls usually start the same way: cooing at dawn, droppings on the patio, a gutter full of feathers and sticks. By the time it's visible from the ground, there's often a full season of nesting packed underneath — which is why we clear existing debris as part of a proper installation rather than sealing a mess in.

What we install, and how

Our critter guard is a PVC-coated wire mesh system custom-fitted around the accessible perimeter of your array:

  • Full-perimeter fit. Every accessible edge sealed — a guard with one open side is a door, not a guard.
  • Warranty-conscious attachment. No drilling into panel frames and no methods manufacturers prohibit. The panels themselves are never modified.
  • Airflow preserved. The mesh closes the entry gap while keeping the ventilation your panels are designed for.
  • Nearly invisible from the street. Fitted tight along the array line, a finished guard disappears into the roofline.

What we don't install: chicken wire, loose plastic netting, or spike strips as a standalone "solution." We've been called to remove plenty of all three. Chicken wire rusts, sags, and can damage panel edges — we wrote the honest comparison in our pigeon-proofing guide — and temporary deterrents mostly relocate birds about six feet. A real guard ends the problem; deterrents negotiate with it.

How you know you need one

  • Cooing, scratching, or fluttering from the roof — especially at dawn
  • Droppings on panels, siding, or the ground below the array
  • Feathers, sticks, or nesting material at the array's edge or washing out of gutters
  • A production dip with no obvious cause (nesting shades cells and damages wiring)
  • Or simply: your array is unguarded, and your neighbors' arrays aren't — the birds on your street have already done the market research

Timing matters, too. A guard installed in fall — before winter sends every pigeon in Denver looking for warm shelter — prevents the problem. The same install in spring can mean clearing an established nest first. Both are doable; the fall version is cheaper and cleaner.

Denver critter guard questions

How much does a critter guard cost in Denver?

A typical residential installation with us runs $1,095–$1,195, confirmed after a photo and roof-access review — and it is backed by our LIFETIME workmanship guarantee. Panel count, array layout, roof pitch, perimeter length, and any existing nesting determine the final scope. Limited repairs to an existing guard start at $300 for a small, accessible section.

Will installing a critter guard void my solar warranty?

Not the way we install it — our attachment method doesn't modify the panels or drill into their frames. The warranty risk lives in improper installs, which is worth asking any company about directly: how does the guard attach, exactly? A vague answer is a red flag.

Is "bird proofing" the same service?

Yes — for solar panels, bird proofing and critter guard installation are the same perimeter mesh job under two names. Homeowners search both, so our pages answer both. The mesh also stops squirrels, which bird-only deterrents often don't.

Can you help if pigeons are already nesting under my panels?

Yes. Clearing the existing nesting and debris is part of a proper installation — we never seal animals in. If young birds are in the nest, we'll talk through humane timing honestly rather than rushing the job.
