Why Lakewood panels get dirty their own way

  • Foothills dust, daily. Green Mountain and the hogback edge shed fine grit on the wind. West Lakewood arrays collect a steady mineral film that rain mostly moves around.
  • An old, tall tree canopy. Central and east Lakewood's mature cottonwoods and pines lay down pollen and sticky residue each spring; it bakes on in the sun and builds along each panel's lower edge.
  • Aging arrays on older roofs. Lakewood was an early solar suburb. Older systems on older roofs accumulate edge debris and lower-row soiling that compounds — the longer since the last real cleaning, the more production is sitting on the table.
  • Hail-corridor storms. Jefferson County takes hail. Storm grit, splashed debris, and the occasional damaged panel are all things a post-season cleaning puts eyes on.

Rain is not a cleaning plan in Lakewood. It spot-dries into mineral marks and leaves the bonded stuff — pollen, droppings, lower-edge residue — exactly where it was.

How we clean: the warranty-safe way

Panel manufacturers are specific about what can touch their glass, and we work the way the manuals say:

  • Pure water — no minerals left behind to spot or film the glass as it dries.
  • Panel-safe soft brushes — no abrasives, nothing that scratches the anti-reflective coating your production depends on.
  • No harsh chemicals — manufacturer-friendly, and nothing running off your roof that you'd mind in your yard.
  • No pressure washers, ever — high-pressure water damages seals and can drive moisture where it doesn't belong.

Done right, cleaning is gentle, thorough, and leaves you photos proving the difference. Done wrong — a pressure washer, a stiff brush, a hardware-store chemical — it can cost you the warranty on a system worth tens of thousands of dollars.

Lakewood cleaning questions

How often should panels be cleaned in Lakewood?

Most Lakewood systems do well with one or two cleanings a year — after the spring pollen season, and again after a dusty or stormy summer. Homes near Green Mountain and low-slope arrays tend toward twice a year; steep, shaded-little roofs can often go longer. Production trend plus a look at the glass is the real guide.

Will cleaning void my panel warranty?

Cleaning it wrong can — manufacturers prohibit abrasive tools, harsh chemicals, and pressure washing. Our method (pure water, soft panel-safe brushes, no chemicals) is the approach the manuals approve, and it matters even more on older systems whose warranties are still in force.
