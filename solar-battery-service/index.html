A straight answer on warranties

Homeowners ask us this a lot: "Who honors my battery warranty if the maker exits the business?" The honest answer is that a manufacturer warranty is only as good as the manufacturer behind it. If a maker is still operating, its warranty follows its own terms, and we can document your battery's condition to support a claim. If a maker has ceased operations, there may be nobody standing behind the product warranty at all — and anyone who promises you otherwise isn't being straight with you. What we can always do is document, review, and tell you exactly where you stand — and coordinate a licensed electrical contractor for hands-on service — so you can make a clear-eyed decision about repair, replacement, or adding new storage. Our own workmanship on any work we perform is covered by our workmanship warranty — that part is ours, and it doesn't depend on anybody else staying in business.

Questions battery owners actually ask

My battery installer went out of business. Can you service my battery?

Yes — that's core work for us. We start with an inspection to document what you have and what condition it's in, then coordinate a licensed electrical contractor for hands-on service. If part of your system can only be handled by the original maker, we'll tell you that plainly and document what we found.

Who honors the warranty if the battery maker exits?

Honestly: a product warranty depends on the maker. If the manufacturer is operating, its warranty follows its own terms, and our documented inspection gives you the condition record a claim starts with. If the maker has ceased operations, there may be no one left to honor it — we won't pretend otherwise. Either way, we can document your battery, review its monitoring, and coordinate licensed service — and our own workmanship warranty covers any work we perform.

The power went out and my battery didn't carry the house. Can you find out why?

Start with an Outage Readiness Check and a documented inspection. Backup behavior depends on state of charge, settings, load, and the battery's actual health — we review the settings and monitoring with you and document what's happening, in plain language. If hands-on electrical testing is needed, that's a licensed electrical contractor's work, and our report gives them a head start.

Can you install a battery on my existing solar system?

Yes — with a licensed electrical contractor doing the installation. Adding storage to an existing array is electrical work; we document your existing system first, help you plan around what you want the battery to do, and coordinate the referral. Installation is quoted per system by the contractor after an inspection, because the right battery and setup depend on your existing equipment and your goals.

How much does battery service cost?

The entry points are published: an Inspection & Report is $245 and an Outage Readiness Check is $149. Hands-on battery service and installation work itself is quoted per system by the licensed electrical contractor after inspection — it depends on the make, model, and condition of your battery, and we'd rather inspect first than invent a figure. Any quote is itemized and approved by you before work begins.

Do you work on batteries from any maker?

We start every battery with an inspection and monitoring review, whatever the badge on the box. Some systems have maker-locked parts or software that limit what any outside company can do; where that's the case, we'll tell you straight, document everything we found, and help you decide on the next step.
