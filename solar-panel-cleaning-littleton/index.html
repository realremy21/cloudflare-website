Why Littleton panels get dirty their own way

  • Foothills grit. Wind coming over the hogback carries fine mineral dust that coats glass evenly — the kind of film you don't notice until production quietly steps down.
  • A real tree canopy. Older Littleton — the ranch homes around Columbine and the streets near historic downtown — has mature cottonwoods and pines. Pollen and sap-adjacent residue bake onto panels in the sun and collect along the lower edge of each module.
  • Low-slope newer roofs. Ken Caryl, Grant Ranch, and the Chatfield-edge subdivisions put a lot of solar on low-pitch roofs. Low tilt means rain redistributes dirt instead of rinsing it, so buildup compounds season over season.
  • Hail-season residue. Littleton sits in the hail corridor. After a storm season, panels carry a mix of grit, leaf litter at the edges, and whatever the last storm left behind — and it's the right moment to look for actual hail damage while cleaning (more on that below).

Rain is not a cleaning plan in Littleton. It spot-dries into mineral marks and leaves the sticky material — pollen, droppings, lower-edge residue — right where it was.

How we clean: the warranty-safe way

Panel manufacturers are specific about what can touch their glass, and we work the way the manuals say:

  • Pure water — no minerals left behind to spot or film the glass as it dries.
  • Panel-safe soft brushes — no abrasives, nothing that scratches the anti-reflective coating your production depends on.
  • No harsh chemicals — manufacturer-friendly, and nothing running off your roof that you'd mind in your yard or garden.
  • No pressure washers, ever — high-pressure water damages seals and can drive moisture where it doesn't belong.

Done wrong — pressure washer, stiff brush, hardware-store chemical — a "cleaning" can cost you the warranty on a system worth tens of thousands of dollars. Done right, it's gentle, thorough, and proven with photos.

Littleton cleaning questions

How often should panels be cleaned in Littleton?

Most Littleton systems do well with one or two cleanings a year — after the spring pollen season, and again in the fall if the summer was dusty or stormy. Homes under heavy cottonwood canopy and low-slope arrays near the foothills tend to need the twice-a-year rhythm.

Will cleaning void my panel warranty?

Cleaning it wrong can — manufacturers prohibit abrasive tools, harsh chemicals, and pressure washing. Our method (pure water, soft panel-safe brushes, no chemicals) is the approach the manuals approve.

Do you check for hail damage during a cleaning?

Yes — cleaning puts us on the roof looking at every panel. If we find hail damage, you'll get date-stamped photos and a written explanation you can hand to your insurer. A full documented Inspection & Report is $245 if you need the formal version for a claim.
