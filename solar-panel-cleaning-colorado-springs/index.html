Why Springs panels get dirty their own way

Every market has its own grime, and the Springs has a particular recipe:

  • Wind-blown grit. Chinook winds and open-plains dust put a fine mineral film on glass that rain mostly redistributes instead of removing.
  • Pollen seasons. Cottonwood and pine pollen stick, bake on in the sun, and build up along the lower edge of each panel — exactly where residue does the most harm.
  • Low-slope newer roofs. A lot of Springs solar went onto the low-pitch roofs of newer subdivisions — Briargate, Stetson Hills, Banning Lewis Ranch, Wolf Ranch. Low tilt means less self-rinsing, so dirt accumulates instead of washing off.
  • Smoke and ash. In a bad wildfire summer, ash and smoke residue settle on arrays across the city and stay until someone removes them.

Rain is not a cleaning plan here. It rinses loose dust, spot dries into mineral marks, and leaves the sticky stuff — pollen, droppings, lower-edge residue — right where it was.

How we clean: the warranty-safe way

Panel manufacturers are specific about what can touch their glass, and we work the way the manuals say:

  • Pure water — no minerals left behind to spot or film the glass as it dries.
  • Panel-safe soft brushes — no abrasives, nothing that scratches the anti-reflective coating your production depends on.
  • No harsh chemicals — manufacturer-friendly, and nothing running off your roof that you'd mind in your yard.
  • No pressure washers, ever — high-pressure water damages seals and can drive moisture where it doesn't belong.

Done right, a cleaning is gentle, thorough, and leaves you with photos proving the difference. Done wrong — a pressure washer, a stiff brush, a hardware-store chemical — it can cost you the warranty on a system worth tens of thousands of dollars. That gap between right and wrong is the whole reason we exist.

When a Springs cleaning is worth it

  • After a smoky summer or a dusty, windy stretch — if your production is off and the glass looks filmed from the roof, that's us.
  • When you can see buildup from the ground — droppings, streaking, or a dull haze on the glass means the film is well past cosmetic.
  • Before you judge your system's production — owners regularly assume a panel or inverter problem that turns out to be dirt. A cleaning and a look at the array map separates the two cheaply.

Colorado Springs cleaning questions

How often should panels be cleaned in Colorado Springs?

Most Springs systems benefit from a cleaning once or twice a year — after the spring pollen and dust season, and again after a smoky or dirty summer if one hits. Low-slope arrays and homes near open land or construction may need it more often. Your production numbers and a look at the glass are the real guide.

Will cleaning void my panel warranty?

Cleaning it wrong can — manufacturers prohibit abrasive tools, harsh chemicals, and pressure washing. Our method (pure water, soft panel-safe brushes, no chemicals) is the approach the manuals approve.

Yes — ground mounts and larger rural arrays are part of our Springs-area work, quoted by size and access after a photo review.
