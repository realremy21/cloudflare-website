Colorado Springs weather is its own thing

Springs arrays deal with a particular mix: hail that can arrive with very little warning, chinook winds that sandblast dust onto the glass all winter, spring snow that lands heavy and wet, and intense high-altitude sun that bakes residue on between storms. Systems here also sit at higher elevation than Denver, which means freeze-thaw cycles work on small hail cracks through the winter — damage that was a watch-and-wait in June can be a failing panel by February. It's why we push a pre-winter inspection for any Springs system that took hail in the spring, and why documentation after every major storm matters more here than almost anywhere else we work.
