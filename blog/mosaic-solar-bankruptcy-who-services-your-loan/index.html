Your Solar Lender Went Bankrupt. Here's Who Services Your Loan Now — and Why Your Panels Still Need Care.

By Jeremy & MeralPublished October 29, 2026

You may have seen the headline: Solar Mosaic, one of the biggest solar lenders in the country, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy. If your panels were financed through Mosaic, your first thought was probably: do I still have to pay for this system? And who do I call when something goes wrong?

Here are the straight answers.

What actually happened

Mosaic filed for Chapter 11 on June 6, 2025, in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas, after funding more than $15 billion in loans for over 500,000 households. The case moved fast. Under the court-approved plan, Mosaic's loan-origination business shut down — and on September 22, 2025, servicing of its entire loan portfolio transferred to Solar Servicing LLC, a subsidiary of Forbright Bank.

What it means for your loan

  • Your loan did not go away. Same interest rate, same payment amount, same schedule. Only the company administering it changed.
  • Your payments and account questions now go to Solar Servicing LLC, not the old Mosaic portal: (866) 493-6367 · loanservicing@solarservicingllc.com
  • A small number of loans were sold to investors who picked a different servicer. If you received a servicing-transition letter, the company named in that letter is the one that counts.

The part most homeowners get wrong

Your lender never held your equipment warranty. Panel and inverter warranties sit with the manufacturers — they're still in force no matter what happened to Mosaic, or to the company that installed your system.

And that's the real problem we see across the Denver metro: since 2022, 91 solar companies have gone bankrupt or exited the business — Mosaic, Sunnova, SunPower, Freedom Forever, and dozens of installers. Thousands of Colorado homeowners are paying every month for systems nobody is cleaning, inspecting, or maintaining, because they assumed a bankruptcy somewhere in the chain meant nobody would touch their roof.

It doesn't work that way. Your system still produces, still gets dirty, still gets visited by squirrels — and it still needs care.

What to do if your lender or installer is gone

  1. Check your latest statement to confirm who services your loan today.
  2. Keep making payments — the loan survives the bankruptcy; stopping hurts your credit, not the lender.
  3. Find your equipment warranty paperwork (panel and inverter brands). That's who backs the hardware.
  4. Get the system looked at. If it hasn't been cleaned or inspected since your installer disappeared, you're likely leaving production — and money — on the table. (If your installer is the one that closed, our companion guide walks through the full checklist.)
