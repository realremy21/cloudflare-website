Solar Detach & Reset in Colorado: What to Know Before You Replace Your Roof

By Jeremy & MeralPublished November 18, 2026

If your home has rooftop solar and your roof needs to be replaced, there's a step in the project that's easy to overlook until it's suddenly urgent: the panels have to come off before the roof does — and they have to go back on right when the new roof is done.

That process is called detach & reset, and it's some of the most consequential work that will ever be done on your solar system. Done well, your system comes through a roof replacement producing exactly like it did before. Done carelessly, you can end up with a system that looks reinstalled but isn't performing — or worse, isn't connected properly at all.

We're Mile High Solar Care, a husband-and-wife solar care company based in Aurora. Detach & reset is core work for us across the Denver metro and the Front Range. Here's what every Colorado homeowner with solar should know before the roofing crew shows up.

What is detach & reset?

Detach & reset (often shortened to D&R) is the complete removal and reinstallation of a rooftop solar system around a roof replacement:

  • Detach — the panels, racking, and wiring are safely removed from the roof so the roofing crew can tear off and install the new roof underneath.
  • Reset — once the new roof is complete, the system is reinstalled, reconnected, and checked to confirm it's producing power again.

It's a solar job, not a roofing job. Your roofer is an expert in roofing; your solar system involves electrical connections, mounting hardware, and production equipment that need a crew whose whole focus is the array itself.

When does a roof replacement require it?

Any time the roofing work requires the array to come off — most commonly a full tear-off and replacement, which is routine in Colorado as hail damage and aging roofs catch up with homes that added solar years ago. If the roofers need clear access to the deck where your panels and mounts sit, the system has to be detached first and reset afterward.

The key is timing. Detach & reset should be scheduled around your roofer's dates, not discovered in the middle of the project. The earlier your solar crew and your roofing crew are coordinated, the smoother the whole replacement goes.

How Mile High Solar Care does detach & reset

Our process is built around one principle: your system should come back exactly as documented, and you should be able to see it producing before we leave.

  1. Documentation first. We photograph the system before, during, and after the work, so there's a clear record of your array's condition and layout at every stage.
  2. Careful detach. Panels, racking, and wiring are removed safely, ahead of your roofer's tear-off date.
  3. Safe storage. Panels are stored safely on site, or hauled and returned — protected for the whole roofing window.
  4. Reset on the new roof. The system is reinstalled on the finished roof and reconnected, with reconnection coordinated alongside our certified-electrician partner so nothing is left half-hooked-up.
  5. Production verified. Before we leave, we confirm the system is generating again. You don't take our word for it — you see your system producing.
  6. The follow-on your system needs. A roof replacement is the perfect moment for a professional cleaning and a full health inspection of the system, plus critter guard where pigeons have moved in under the array. We can fold those in while we're already on the roof.

The warranty difference — ask about it in writing

Here's the part most homeowners never think to ask about, and it's the part that matters most years later.

Detach & reset work in this market is typically backed by a workmanship warranty of about five years. When that clock runs out, it runs out — and if the home is sold, a short, non-transferable warranty may do the next owner no good at all.

Every detach & reset we perform is backed by our transferable lifetime installation warranty. It doesn't expire on a five-year clock, and it transfers to the next homeowner if you sell your house. If we put our name on the installation, we stand behind it for the life of that installation.

Whatever company you hire — including us — get the warranty answer in writing before the work starts.

How to vet a detach & reset provider

Before you hand anyone your roof and your solar system, ask these questions:

  • How long is your installation warranty — and is it transferable? Then get the answer in writing.
  • Will you verify production before you leave? A reinstalled system should be confirmed generating, not just bolted back down.
  • Do you document the system with photos before, during, and after? You deserve a record of what was done.
  • Who handles the electrical reconnection? Reconnection should be coordinated with a certified electrician, not improvised.
  • Do you work around my roofer's schedule? The detach and reset dates should be built around the tear-off and the new roof, not the other way around.
  • Who actually does the work? We don't install new solar systems and we don't do roofing — detach & reset, cleaning, inspections, diagnostics, and critter guards are our trade, and it's the work we stand behind.
