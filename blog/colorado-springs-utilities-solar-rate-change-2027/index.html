Colorado Springs Utilities Is Changing the Solar Rules on April 1, 2027. The Real Deadline Is the One Before It.

By Jeremy & MeralPublished November 9, 2026

If your home has solar — or you're thinking about getting it — and Colorado Springs Utilities is your electric utility, September 22, 2026 quietly became one of the most important dates on your calendar. That's the day CSU adopted Resolutions 81-26 and 82-26, changing the solar terms for its customers. The new terms take effect April 1, 2027.

We're Jeremy and Meral Runnels, the husband-and-wife team behind Mile High Solar Care. We clean, inspect, and maintain solar systems across the Front Range, including a growing number of homes in Colorado Springs. Rate-rule changes like this one land in our customers' mailboxes as fine print, so here's the plain-English version: what changed, who it hits, and the one deadline that actually matters.

What CSU decided

Under the new terms adopted in Resolutions 81-26 and 82-26, solar agreements that begin on or after April 1, 2027 run under a new, less favorable structure. For the average affected customer, CSU's change works out to roughly $38 more per month. That's about $456 a year — every year — for the same sunshine and the same panels.

Nothing about your hardware changes. What changes is the deal: how your system is credited and what it costs you under the new terms.

The grandfather clause is the whole story

Buried in the change is the most important sentence for Colorado Springs solar owners: agreements signed before the new terms take effect keep the current terms until April 1, 2032.

Read that again. If your solar agreement is in place before April 1, 2027, the current — better — terms are locked in for five more years. If it isn't, you're on the new terms from day one, at roughly $38 a month more on average. Across that same five-year window, that's a difference of roughly $2,280.

Deadlines that lock in five years of better terms don't come around often. This one is worth circling.

What to do before April 1, 2027

If you're considering solar: the date that matters is the date your agreement is signed — not the install date, not the day the panels first produce. If solar is on your 2027 list anyway, getting the agreement done before April 1, 2027 puts you under the current terms until 2032. Talk to installers early; spring books fill up, and a signed agreement in February beats a rushed one in late March.

If you already have solar: you're very likely grandfathered — but "very likely" isn't the same as knowing. Confirm your agreement date and status with Colorado Springs Utilities so there's a record that your system sits under the current terms. Five minutes on the phone now beats an argument about a bill in 2028.

Either way, make sure the system earns its keep. Grandfathered terms are only worth what your panels actually produce. A system that's dirty, shaded by new growth, hosting pigeons, or quietly underperforming wastes the very years you just locked in. The run-up to April 2027 is the right moment for a real health check — production verified, panels clean, critters out — so the system enters the grandfather window at full strength.

FAQ

I already have solar with CSU. Does the April 2027 change raise my bill?

Not under the grandfather provision: agreements signed before the new terms take effect keep the current terms until April 1, 2032. Confirm your agreement status with CSU so it's on record.

I'm thinking about going solar in 2027. When do I need to act?

Your agreement needs to be signed before April 1, 2027 to land under the current terms. Start the conversation with an installer well before spring — the signed date is the one that counts.

Does this affect Xcel Energy customers?

No. This change is Colorado Springs Utilities only. Xcel customers have their own rules — including a separate December 1 Solar Bank deadline we wrote up in our Xcel Solar Bank election guide.

Do you install solar?

No — we clean, inspect, guard, and maintain it. If you need an installer, get your agreement signed before the deadline; then we'll happily keep the system producing the way it should.
