Plug-In Solar Just Got Its First Colorado Helper. Here's the Honest Version.

By Jeremy & MeralPublished November 11, 2026

The biggest protections in Colorado's new plug-in solar law don't kick in until January 1, 2027. The first organization built to help people use it just showed up anyway.

This week, a new Denver-based nonprofit called Solar Access Colorado launched with pre-orders for small plug-in solar kits — the kind designed for balconies, patios, and fences. It bills itself as the state's first organization dedicated to plug-in and balcony solar, and it's aimed squarely at the people rooftop solar has always left out: renters, apartment and multifamily residents, and homeowners whose roofs just don't work.

We're Jeremy and Meral Runnels, the husband-and-wife team behind Mile High Solar Care in Aurora. We don't sell plug-in kits, and we don't sell rooftop systems either — we clean, inspect, and maintain solar for Colorado homes. We spent Monday at the Colorado PUC testifying on the meter collar rules at the heart of this law's rollout. So here's what this launch actually means, and what it doesn't.

What Solar Access Colorado is doing

Founder Adam Harris launched the nonprofit to help people understand the new rules and sort through product and installation requirements as the law takes effect. Beyond selling kits, it plans to work with municipalities, housing organizations, property managers, and community groups, with education and outreach focused on low-income and multifamily households.

That's the right instinct. Plug-in solar only helps the people it was written for if somebody trustworthy explains it in plain English.

What the law actually says — and when

Colorado's plug-in solar law (HB 26-1007) creates a framework for small, portable solar devices. Beginning January 1, 2027, qualifying systems get protection from unreasonable restrictions on installation and use — including from HOAs, which have to treat these devices as energy-efficiency measures.

Three honest caveats:

  1. The key protections are not live yet. The ban on unreasonable restrictions — including from HOAs — starts January 1, 2027, and the PUC's own rules for the equipment at the center of this — meter collar adapters — aren't due until December 31, 2026. A pre-order is a place in line, not a blank check to plug in today.
  2. Safety rules still apply. The law allows reasonable safety restrictions — secure mounting, fire prevention, electrical overload, and property damage are all still fair game. A kit dangling off a balcony railing is not what this law protects.
  3. Small means small. Colorado caps plug-in systems at 1,920 watts, one system per address — and the biggest systems need a licensed electrician and a dedicated circuit, while the smallest tier (391 watts and under) skips the wiring requirements entirely. That's a real offset for daytime use — it is not a whole-home power plant, and anyone who sells it as one is telling you something.

Before you pre-order anything, check the label

This is the same thing we said in September, and it matters more now that pre-orders are open: check the certification before you buy. Colorado's law requires these devices to be labeled and listed by a nationally recognized testing lab — and starting January 1, 2027, selling a device over 391 watts in Colorado without that UL listing is flat-out prohibited. UL 3700 is the safety standard written for exactly these systems. If a seller can't show you the certification, that's your answer.

And check your own situation before money moves: your lease, your HOA's actual rules, and whether your balcony or patio gets sun worth harvesting. A north-facing balcony in the shade is a bad investment at any price.

Why we're glad this exists

Most of the homes we work on have rooftop systems somebody else installed and walked away from. Plug-in solar is a different door into the same house: people taking their own power seriously, at a size they can afford. If Solar Access Colorado does this with integrity — honest expectations, certified gear, no plugging in early — Colorado renters finally get a seat at the table they've been watching through the window.

We'll be watching how the pre-orders and the PUC rules land, and we'll update this post as they do. If you're weighing a plug-in kit and want a second set of eyes that has nothing to sell you, reach out. That's kind of our whole thing.
