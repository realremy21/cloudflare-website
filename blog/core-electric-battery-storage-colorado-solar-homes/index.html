CORE Electric Is Building Grid Batteries in Central Colorado. Here's What That Means If You Have Solar.

By Jeremy & MeralPublished October 27, 2026

If your power comes from CORE Electric Cooperative, here's a piece of news worth knowing about — even though nothing on your bill changes today.

On October 26, Madison Energy Infrastructure announced it's building a 50-megawatt / 200-megawatt-hour battery storage portfolio with CORE, spread across three sites in Colorado. The batteries are Tesla Megapacks, and they're expected to be up and running by September 2027. Madison calls it their largest distributed storage project so far. CORE serves more than 375,000 people across about 5,000 square miles of central Colorado, so this is our backyard, not somewhere far off.

Why a utility builds a giant battery

Your panels make the most power in the middle of the day. Your neighborhood uses the most power in the evening — cooking, laundry, AC winding down, everybody home at once. That gap is the whole story of solar in 2026.

A battery this size soaks up power when there's plenty and lets it back out when demand spikes. In CORE's own words, the goal is to "manage peak demand, control power costs, and maintain reliable service" as new homes and businesses keep getting built around us. That's it. No mystery — it's the same reason a homeowner pairs a battery with rooftop solar, just at neighborhood scale.

What this does not change

We want to be straight with you, because battery headlines tend to get oversold:

  • Your net metering doesn't change. This announcement isn't a rate case and it doesn't touch how CORE credits the solar you send to the grid.
  • Your bill doesn't change because of this. Not this month, not next. The project isn't even operating until fall 2027.
  • Nobody needs to do anything. There's no sign-up, no program to join, nothing to buy.

What it does mean for solar homes

Two honest takeaways:

1. Storage is where the grid is headed. When your own co-op is spending on batteries to handle the evening peak, it tells you where the value of solar power is shifting — sunshine at noon is plentiful; power at 7 p.m. is precious. If you're ever weighing a home battery down the road, this is the trend that decision will sit inside. We're a cleaning and care company, not battery installers, so we won't sell you one — but we're happy to tell you what we're seeing.

2. Your panels still have to do their job. Grid batteries don't help a dirty or underperforming array. Every bit of power your system doesn't make because of dust, pollen, bird mess, or an undiagnosed problem is power you buy back at retail. Keeping panels clean and inspected is still the cheapest production you own — that part hasn't changed, and it's the part we can actually help with.

The bottom line

CORE and Madison are building storage because Colorado keeps growing and the evening peak keeps getting more expensive to serve. For solar owners in CORE country, it's a good sign for grid reliability — and a reminder that your system's job is to be ready, clean and producing, every sunny day between now and when those Megapacks switch on in 2027.
