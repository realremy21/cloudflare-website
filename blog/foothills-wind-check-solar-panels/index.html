The Foothills Took 75 MPH Wind Last Night. Here's the Solar Check to Do From the Ground.

By Jeremy & MeralPublished November 18, 2026

If you woke up in Denver this morning to sunshine and calm air, your panels are fine — this one wasn't yours. The storm that came through overnight was a foothills storm. The National Weather Service had a High Wind Warning up until 11 this morning for the northern Front Range foothills — the country between about 6,000 and 9,000 feet, places like Nederland, Estes Park, and Red Feather Lakes — with west winds gusting 70 to 75 miles an hour. That's enough wind to bring down trees and power lines, and it's enough to be worth a look at your roof today.

We're Jeremy and Meral with Mile High Solar Care. We clean, inspect, and look after solar systems across the Front Range, and after a wind night like that, this is the check we wish every foothills homeowner would do. It takes five minutes, and all of it happens with your feet on the ground.

1. Walk the yard and look up. From the ground, look for anything that doesn't belong: a branch resting on the array, debris piled against the panels, or anything visibly shifted or hanging loose along the edge of the system. Wind works on edges first — that's where critter guard mesh and trim live — so a section that looks pushed, bent, or open is worth noting.

2. Check your monitoring app before you check anything else. A sunny morning like today should show your system waking up and producing. If the app shows zero production, an error, or that the system went offline during an outage and never came back, that's real information. Write down what it says — a photo or screenshot of the error helps whoever you call.

3. Take ground-level photos, even if everything looks fine. A few phone photos of each side of the array, dated today, are the start of a record. If something does turn up later — a production dip, a leak, a displaced section — you'll be glad you can show what the roof looked like right after the storm.

What not to do: don't climb up there. Not during the wind, and not the morning after, when you're looking for damage you can't actually assess from a ladder anyway. And if you ever see wiring hanging loose or damaged, don't touch it — that's a call-a-pro call, full stop.

Call a pro if: you can see visible damage or displaced equipment from the ground, your monitoring shows an error that doesn't clear or a sunny-day production drop, or your power was out and the system didn't restart with the grid. Those are inspection items, not wait-and-see items.

Most foothills systems will come through a night like this without a scratch — they're built and mounted for Colorado wind. The check isn't about worrying. It's about catching the one loose edge or quiet error this week, while it's small, instead of finding it on a power bill three months from now.

— Jeremy & Meral Runnels, Mile High Solar Care
