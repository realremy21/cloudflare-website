Pigeon Proofing Solar Panels in Denver: Why a Real Critter Guard Works (and Chicken Wire Doesn't)

By Jeremy & MeralPublished October 29, 2026

If you have rooftop solar in the Denver metro, there's a good chance pigeons have already found it. We're Jeremy and Meral Runnels, the husband-and-wife team behind Mile High Solar Care here in Aurora, and pigeon proofing solar panels in Denver is one of the most common reasons people call us. Not because the panels failed — because the space underneath them is the best bird shelter in the neighborhood.

Here's what's going on under your array, why the DIY fixes don't hold, and what a proper critter guard install looks like.

Why pigeons love your solar panels

A rooftop array sits a few inches above the roof, shaded, warm, and protected from wind, rain, and most predators. To a pigeon, that's not a power plant — that's a covered porch built just for nesting. Once one pair moves in, others follow, and a small problem becomes a colony fast. That colony causes real damage:

Droppings everywhere. Pigeon droppings pile up on the panels, the roof, and everything below. On the glass, they shade cells and cut into production. On the roof and in the gutters, they build up into a mess that holds moisture — and the dawn noise on a bedroom roof wears people down.

Nesting debris under the array. Twigs, feathers, and nesting material pack in under the panels and collect leaves and dirt with them, trapping moisture against your roof — exactly where your wiring runs.

Wire damage. The same sheltered space attracts squirrels too, and critters chew. Chewed wire insulation is one of the more expensive surprises we document — a system can look fine from the ground while a string is offline from damage you can't see.

It doesn't resolve on its own. Pigeons return to the same nesting site season after season. Scaring them off works for about a week; if nothing physical blocks the space, they come back — usually with friends.

Why DIY chicken wire fails

We see this on roofs all over Aurora and Denver: chicken wire or hardware cloth wrapped around the array. It looks like the same idea. It isn't, and here's why it usually fails within a season or two:

  • The openings are wrong. Poultry wire is made to keep chickens in, not pigeons out. Determined birds push and bend their way past it — and once one gets in, the wire can make it harder to get out.
  • It sags and gaps. Flimsy wire doesn't hold its shape around corners, conduit entries, and roof transitions. A guard only works if every gap is closed; birds only need one.
  • It rusts and stains. Cheap uncoated wire corrodes fast in Colorado's sun-and-snow cycle and leaves rust streaks on your roof.
  • It gets screwed into things it shouldn't touch. Many DIY jobs are fastened with screws into the roof or panel frames — roof penetration you never needed, potential leak points, and the kind of modification panel manufacturers don't want to see if a warranty question comes up.

Chicken wire is cheaper because it's a temporary version of the job. Most people who try it end up paying for the real install anyway, after a season of the birds winning.

What a proper critter guard install looks like

Solar panel bird proofing in Aurora and across the metro comes down to the right material, fitted properly, with the whole perimeter closed. Our process:

  1. Inspect and photograph first. We document what's under your array — nesting, debris, any damage — so you can see exactly what we found.
  2. Clean out and haul away. All nests and debris come out and leave with us. If we find live young, they're safely rehomed — relocated to Greenwood Wildlife Rehabilitation Center in Longmont. Nothing gets harmed on one of our jobs; that's a rule, not a slogan.
  3. Fit real solar mesh. We use YESON solar panel guard mesh — galvanized steel wire with a black PVC coating and 1/2-inch openings, cut to fit your specific array. The small openings keep pigeons and squirrels out while air still flows under the panels as it should.
  4. Clip it to the frame — no drilling. Stainless steel clips fasten the mesh directly to the array frame. No drilling into panels, no screws into your roof, no roof penetration at all.
  5. Photograph the finished job. Before-and-after photos on every home, so you can see the closed perimeter yourself.

That combination — steel mesh that holds its shape, clips that penetrate nothing, and a perimeter with no gaps at corners or conduit — makes it a one-time fix instead of an annual argument with the pigeons.

FAQ

How do I know if birds are under my panels?

Listen at dawn — scratching and cooing on the roof is the giveaway. You'll also see droppings on the panels and below the array's edge, nesting material washing into the gutters, and sometimes a production dip you can't explain.

Will a critter guard hurt the birds?

No. It's exclusion, not a trap — birds simply can't get under the array, so they nest somewhere else. Any young already under there get rehomed to Greenwood, not removed.

Can I install a guard if birds are already nesting?

Yes — that's most of our calls. We clean out the nesting first, then guard the array so they can't come back.
