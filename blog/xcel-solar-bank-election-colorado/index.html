The Xcel Solar Bank Election: A December 1 Deadline Most Colorado Solar Owners Have Never Heard Of

By Jeremy & MeralPublished November 9, 2026

Somewhere in the stack of paperwork you e-signed when your solar system was installed, there was a form most installers never explained: Xcel Energy's Solar Bank Election Form. That one checkbox decides what happens to every excess kilowatt-hour your panels produce — and if you never chose, Xcel chose for you.

We're Jeremy and Meral Runnels, the husband-and-wife team behind Mile High Solar Care here in Aurora. We read customers' Xcel bills every week, and this election is the most common surprise hiding in them. It carries a December 1 deadline, so here's the honest version: what it is, what each option does, and how to check which one you're on.

What the Solar Bank actually is

When your panels produce more than your home uses, the extra flows to the grid and Xcel tracks it — building up in what's effectively a bank. The Solar Bank Election is your instruction for what Xcel does with that banked energy. There are two options.

Option A: Continuous Rollover

Under Continuous Rollover, your excess generation becomes a dollar credit at the total per-kWh energy rate in effect for the time period the power was generated — the same energy rate you're charged when you buy electricity. The credit wipes out energy charges on your current bill (everything except the fixed Service and Facilities charge), and whatever is left rolls forward month after month. On your bill, it shows up as "Rollover Bank Dollar Credit." Summer's extra production quietly pays your winter bills.

Rollover is also permanent — Xcel's form says it "cannot be changed under any circumstances," and if you move or stop service, the credits are gone; they can't transfer to your home's buyer.

Option B: Waive Decision — the default

If no option was ever chosen — and for a lot of Colorado systems, nobody chose — your account was automatically set to Waive Decision.

Under Waive, your excess still rolls month to month during the year, but at year-end Xcel empties the bank and mails you a check. The catch: they buy that power at the "average hourly incremental cost of electricity," or AHIC — Xcel's wholesale cost of energy, not your retail rate. Xcel's own form lists what AHIC has paid:

  • 2023: 1.529¢ per kWh
  • 2022: 2.672¢ per kWh
  • 2021: 1.786¢ per kWh
  • 2020: 1.115¢ per kWh
  • 2019: 1.205¢ per kWh
  • 2018: 1.503¢ per kWh

Run the math: a system that banks 1,000 extra kWh in a year — not unusual for a well-sized Front Range array — gets a year-end check of about $15.29 at 2023's rate. Under Rollover, those same 1,000 kWh offset 1,000 kWh of energy charges on future bills — roughly ten times as much for most homes. Same sunshine, same panels. The only difference is a checkbox.

Who should think twice before switching

Rollover is right for most people who plan to stay in their home — but not everyone, and we'd rather be honest than loud:

  • Planning to move in the next few years? Rollover credits vanish when you stop service; Waive at least gets you a (small) check every year. Just bought a home with solar? The previous owner's credits didn't come with the house — but you can make your own election.
  • On Budget Billing? Xcel notes Rollover may remove you from Budget Billing, because the credits can distort the averaged amount.
  • Rollover is forever. There is no switching back. Waive preserves one thing: a one-time option to move to Rollover at any point during your contract.

The December 1 deadline, and how to check yours

The switch is available any time — but Xcel's form states it "must be made no later than December 1 for the option to apply to the current year." Miss December 1, 2026, and this year's bank still cashes out at AHIC prices; Rollover would only help from next year forward.

To check where you stand:

  1. Look at a recent Xcel bill. If you see a "Rollover Bank Dollar Credit" line, you're on Rollover already — you're done.
  2. No rollover line, and your system regularly produces more than you use? You're very likely on the Waive default.
  3. To switch, complete Xcel's Solar Bank Election Form and email it to SolarProgramCO@xcelenergy.com before December 1. (Use the current form from Xcel's website — older copies show an outdated fax number and email.)

One more option: under either election, you can direct your year-end excess credits to Energy Outreach Colorado, which distributes them to income-qualified Colorado households. That choice has the same December 1 cutoff.

FAQ

I don't remember signing anything about a Solar Bank. Which one am I on?

Almost certainly Waive — it's the automatic default. Check your bill for the "Rollover Bank Dollar Credit" line to confirm.

If I switch to Rollover, can I change my mind later?

No — Rollover is permanent. That's why it's worth a few minutes of thought before you file, not a panic on November 30.

Does this apply to my Colorado Springs Utilities / Black Hills / co-op system?

No — this election is specific to Xcel Energy (Public Service Company of Colorado) net metered customers. Other Colorado utilities run their own net metering rules.

The short version

If you're an Xcel customer with solar, find out which election you're on this week. If you're on the Waive default and planning to stay put, every year you wait is a year of sunshine cashed out at wholesale pennies. The form takes minutes; the deadline is December 1.
