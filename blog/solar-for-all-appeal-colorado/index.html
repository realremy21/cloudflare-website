Solar for All Update: The EPA Appealed — Colorado's $156 Million Is Still Frozen

By Jeremy & MeralPublished November 20, 2026

In September, a federal judge ruled that the EPA had unlawfully shut down Solar for All, the $7 billion program meant to bring rooftop and community solar to low-income households. It was a real win, and we wrote about it here because Colorado's share — $156 million — would have put solar within reach for a lot of families who've never had the option.

This week, the story took a turn. On Tuesday, November 17, the EPA appealed that ruling to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the First Circuit. The appeal asks the court to undo the order that vacated the program's termination — in plain terms, the government is fighting to keep the program shut down.

What this means for Colorado right now

Nothing has opened up, and nothing is about to. The Colorado Energy Office still lists our state's Solar for All program as temporarily on hold. There are no applications to fill out, no waitlist to join, and no installers being assigned — about 75% of Colorado's $156 million was earmarked as direct financial assistance for low-income households, and every dollar of it is frozen while the lawyers argue.

Appeals like this take months, sometimes longer. Anyone who was counting on Solar for All to make a system affordable in 2026 should plan as if it isn't coming this year — and be pleasantly surprised if that changes.

One warning worth repeating

When a big program is in the news, the scams follow. If someone knocks on your door or calls offering to "reserve your spot" in Solar for All, sign you up early, or hold your place for a fee — that's not how this works, and it's not real. There is no application, so there is nothing to reserve. The Colorado Energy Office will announce it loudly, through official channels, if and when the program ever opens.

What you can actually do today

  • If you already have solar: your system doesn't care what's happening in court. Panels that are dirty, shaded by debris, or losing a fight with pigeons produce less power every single day, and no ruling changes that math. Keeping the system you own running at full production is the one solar win fully in your control.
  • If you're shopping for solar: get quotes based on today's real numbers, not a promised program. Ask any installer, in writing, exactly which incentives your quote assumes — and what the price is if those incentives never arrive.
  • If cost is the barrier: check the Colorado Energy Office and your own utility's website directly for what's currently open. Programs change fast right now, and the official pages are the only source we'd trust.
