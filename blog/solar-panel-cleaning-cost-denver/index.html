By Jeremy & MeralPublished October 21, 2026

If you own solar in the Denver metro, you've probably wondered whether paying someone to clean your panels is worth it — or whether the rain handles it for free. Here's the honest version: sometimes cleaning is clearly worthwhile, sometimes it honestly isn't, and a good operator should tell you which one you're looking at before you spend anything.

When cleaning may be worthwhile

Visible soiling. If you can see a film of dirt, dust, or pollen on the glass from the ground, that layer is sitting between the sun and your cells. Colorado's dry climate means we get long stretches without a washing rain, and the grime builds up quietly.

Pollen and dust season. Spring pollen and summer dust are the Front Range's two big soiling seasons. A cleaning after the heavy pollen drops — and before the high-production summer months — is a well-timed visit.

After a storm. Hail season and windstorms leave more than dirt behind: leaves, twigs, and debris pile up along the bottom edge of arrays and behind panels. That debris holds moisture against the roof and gives birds and squirrels a reason to move in. A post-storm cleaning doubles as a look at the whole array while we're up there.

When your monitoring says something's off. If your production app shows a drop you can't explain, soiling is one of the cheap things to rule out — and a cleaning visit includes a visual check of the array, which may reveal visible concerns that warrant further assessment — a cracked panel, a loose guard, or a nesting problem. The visual check does not establish electrical safety or replace diagnostics.

Before a warranty or monitoring check. If you're documenting your system's condition — for a warranty question, a home sale, or your own records — a clean array photographs and inspects properly. Cleaning can make some visible conditions easier to document.

When it honestly doesn't

We'll say this plainly, because we'd rather earn the next visit than oversell this one:

  • If your panels look clean and your production is normal, a cleaning is maintenance, not a rescue. You may not get your money's worth that day.
  • If it just rained hard, wait. A real downpour does part of the job. (A light sprinkle, on the other hand, mostly turns dust into spots.)
  • If your array is new, it likely doesn't need us yet. Most new systems in the Denver area are fine for their first stretch unless a storm or construction dust got to them.
  • If a quote promises a specific production jump, be skeptical. We don't promise percentage gains, because honest operators can't know them for your roof, your tilt, and your dirt. What we can promise is clean glass, a checked array, and photos that show you the difference.

That last point matters to us: our whole business runs on people calling us back. Telling you "not yet" when that's the truth is how that works.

What a cleaning visit includes

  • A look at your monitoring, if you'd like, to review available monitoring for obvious alerts or reporting gaps
  • A heads-up on anything else we spot, like debris buildup or signs of critters under the array

Plenty of our customers pair a cleaning with the other upkeep that comes with owning solar — a critter guard inspection, a general system inspection, or a monitoring check. If something else needs attention, we'll tell you what we found and what it would cost. No pressure either way.

FAQ

How often should I clean my solar panels in Colorado?

Once a year is a sensible rhythm, timed after spring pollen or before summer production peaks. Homes near construction, busy roads, or lots of trees may benefit from a second visit. If your monitoring looks healthy and the glass looks clear, you can stretch it — we'll tell you honestly if we think you can.

How much does solar panel cleaning cost in Aurora?

The same published pricing as Denver: $129 for up to 12 panels, $179 for 13–24 panels, $249 for 25–40, plus $50 if two-story access applies. We confirm the price before we start, and the price we give you is the price you pay when we're done.

Can't I just hose them off myself?

You can rinse loose dust off, and it helps a little. What it doesn't do is reach the film that builds up along panel edges, or give you a safe way to inspect the array up close — and getting on a roof with a hose has its own risks. If you do rinse them yourself, use plain water, no detergents, and never walk on the panels.

Will cleaning void my panel warranty?

Follow your panel manufacturer's cleaning instructions and warranty terms. Approved methods can vary by model. Avoid pressure washers, abrasive pads, and harsh chemicals, which can damage panels.

Do you charge by the panel?

We price by panel-count range, not a per-panel meter: $129 for up to 12 panels, $179 for 13–24, $249 for 25–40, with roof height and access confirmed up front. You get one flat number before we start.

The short version

If your panels are visibly dirty, your production has dipped, or a storm just rolled through, a cleaning may be worthwhile — or at least worth a closer assessment. If your array looks clean and your app looks happy, keep your money until one of those things changes.
