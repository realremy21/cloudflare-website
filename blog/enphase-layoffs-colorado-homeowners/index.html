Enphase Is Cutting 320 Jobs. If Your Panels Run on Enphase, Here's What That Actually Means.

By Jeremy & MeralPublished November 22, 2026

On Friday, November 20, Enphase Energy told its employees — and the SEC — that it will cut about 320 jobs, roughly 12% of everyone who works there. Some jobs will move to lower-cost regions overseas. The company expects the changes to be mostly done in the first half of 2027.

We service a lot of Enphase systems across the Front Range, so we read the filing the day it landed. Here's the plain version.

Why Enphase is shrinking

Enphase said it plainly: in the third quarter of this year, U.S. sell-through fell 35% compared to a year earlier. The big reason is one Colorado homeowners already know — the federal 30% Residential Clean Energy Credit ended on December 31, 2025. Fewer new systems being sold means less revenue, and Enphase is cutting its quarterly operating expenses from about $80 million to $60–65 million, starting in the first quarter of 2027, to match.

This is a demand story, not a product-failure story. Nothing in the filing says Enphase equipment is defective, recalled, or being discontinued.

What has NOT changed — and this matters

  • Your warranty has not changed. Enphase did not announce any change to its warranty terms. If your microinverters carry Enphase's 25-year warranty, that is still what the paperwork says today.
  • Your system keeps working the same way. Panels and microinverters on your roof don't phone headquarters to make electricity. A smaller head office doesn't dim your production.
  • Enphase is not closing. This is a restructuring — a company shrinking to fit a smaller market — not a bankruptcy or a shutdown. We know the difference well; we've written here about the installers who actually did disappear, and this isn't that.

What could change, honestly

When a manufacturer cuts 12% of its people and moves some work overseas, the thing homeowners usually feel first isn't the warranty — it's the wait. Support hold times, case turnaround, and replacement-part shipping can get slower while a company reorganizes, especially in the first half of 2027 while these changes roll out. Enphase hasn't promised that will happen, and we won't pretend it's certain. It's just the pattern we've seen when big solar companies restructure.

What we'd do if it were our roof

  1. Save your paperwork now. Your installation contract, your Enphase warranty details, and a screenshot of your system in the Enphase app — while everything is easy to pull up. If you ever need a claim, having your own copies beats asking a reorganizing company to find them.
  2. Check that your monitoring is actually reporting. A system that stopped reporting months ago is a problem you'll want found now, not during a support backlog. If your app shows old data, that's worth a look.
  3. Don't panic-buy or panic-switch anything. There is nothing to replace and nothing to upgrade because of this news. Anyone who calls you this week saying your Enphase warranty is "about to be worthless" is selling you something.
  4. Know who your local option is. If Enphase support ever does get slow, an independent local service company can diagnose your system, document what's wrong, and handle the warranty conversation with evidence in hand. That's most of what we do every week — for Enphase systems and every other brand on Colorado roofs.

The bottom line

Enphase got smaller this week because America is buying fewer new solar systems since the tax credit ended — not because the equipment on your roof stopped being good. Your warranty stands as written. Keep your records, keep an eye on your monitoring, and if something does go wrong with your system, you don't have to sit on hold alone.
