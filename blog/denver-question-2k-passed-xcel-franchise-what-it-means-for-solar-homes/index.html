Denver Said Yes to Xcel's Next 20 Years — Here's What That Means for Your Solar

By Jeremy & MeralPublished November 6, 2026

Denver voters have decided. Question 2K — the 20-year franchise agreement between the City and County of Denver and Xcel Energy — passed on Tuesday, November 3, with about 68% of the vote: 138,842 yes to 63,888 no, in the count reported as of 11:30 Tuesday night by the Colorado Secretary of State and the Denver Elections Division. Those numbers are unofficial until the results are certified, but the margin isn't close.

We stayed neutral before the vote, and we're not here to re-litigate it now. We're here to do what we did in our pre-vote explainer: sort out what this actually does — and doesn't — change for a Denver home with solar on the roof.

What Denver just approved

  • A 20-year franchise starting January 1, 2027 and running through the end of 2046. It replaces the current agreement, which expires at the end of this year.
  • Xcel keeps its right to use Denver's streets, alleys, and rights-of-way to deliver electricity and gas — and keeps paying the city for that access: a franchise fee of 3% of its Denver revenue, roughly 32 to 34 million dollars a year. That fee is collected on customer bills, same as it is today.
  • The program to bury overhead power lines continues, along with the infrastructure-safety and emergency-coordination commitments in the ballot language, and the companion energy partnership agreement between the city and Xcel takes effect alongside the franchise.
  • The deal also sets aside climate money. Denver secured a carve-out of 2 million dollars a year inside the franchise for climate programs — the kind of funding that goes toward things like heat pumps and electric vehicles. It's a small line next to the franchise fee, but it's real money, pointed at exactly the upgrades Denver homes are making alongside their solar.

What this does NOT change for solar homeowners

This is the part that matters most for our customers, and it hasn't moved an inch since before the election:

  • It does not set your electric rates. Rates are decided through the Colorado Public Utilities Commission, not in a city franchise vote. Nothing about Tuesday's result raises or lowers your bill by itself.
  • It does not change net metering, solar incentives, or your interconnection. How your panels are credited for the power they send to the grid is exactly what it was on Monday.
  • You don't need to do anything. No forms, no calls, no changes to your system. Your solar keeps working the same way it did before the election.

What it means in plain terms

For the next 20 years, Denver and Xcel are locked into a known arrangement instead of a renegotiation. For a solar home, the practical upside is boring, and boring is good: the utility relationship underneath your interconnection is settled, and the work of burying overhead lines — the lines that come down in wind and ice and take neighborhoods dark — keeps funded. The 2 million dollars a year in climate funding also gives the city a small but steady pot for the home-energy upgrades that pair naturally with rooftop solar. The arguments critics made about the length of the term and the city's leverage along the way are now the city's to manage at the agreement's regular check-ins; none of them land on your roof.

One neighbor note: this vote was Denver's only. If you're in Broomfield or Glendale, your cities' Xcel agreements run through the end of 2027 — your turn, if it comes, comes later.

The bottom line for solar homes

The election is over, the franchise is settled through 2046, and nothing about how your system produces, how you're credited, or who takes care of your panels changed on Tuesday night. That last part is still us.
