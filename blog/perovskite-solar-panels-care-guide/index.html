Perovskite Solar Panels Are Coming. Nobody Knows How to Care for Them Yet.

By Jeremy & MeralPublished November 26, 2026

A new kind of solar panel is moving from the lab toward rooftops. Perovskite — and especially perovskite-silicon "tandem" panels, which stack a perovskite layer on top of a conventional silicon cell — can convert more sunlight to electricity than silicon alone. Tandem PV, one of the companies leading this work, plans to ship field test kits in late 2026 so real panels can be tested on real buildings in real weather.

That is genuinely exciting. Colorado rooftops could one day produce meaningfully more power from the same square footage.

But at the RE+ industry conference this year, manufacturers said something worth hearing plainly: there is no durability playbook for perovskite yet. These modules have not spent 25 years on roofs. Nobody yet knows exactly how they age in hail, heat, freeze-thaw cycles, and high-altitude sun — and nobody has published care guidance for them. The cleaning and maintenance methods proven safe on silicon panels for decades cannot simply be assumed safe on a different material.

Here is what that means if you own solar in Colorado today: nothing changes. Your silicon system has a quarter-century of field history and well-understood care. This is about the next technology, not yours.

And here is what it means for us. Mile High Solar Care has a standing company rule: we never service a module technology without the manufacturer's published care guidance for that technology. When perovskite panels reach Colorado roofs — test sites first, early adopters after — we will already have been watching the durability research and the care guidance as it develops, and we will service those systems the way their manufacturers specify, not the way silicon taught us.

New technology is good for solar. Honest maintenance is how it lasts. We will be ready for both.

— Mile High Solar Care, Aurora, Colorado
